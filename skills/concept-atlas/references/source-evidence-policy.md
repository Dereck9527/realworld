# Source and evidence policy

## Research mode

Use `verified` only when browsing is available, permitted, and the researcher has opened/read sources supporting consequential claims. Origin, intellectual-history, and current-use claims require search/browsing in that situation. Use `provisional` when browsing is unavailable or prohibited; say which limitation applies and never present it as verified.

## Source selection

Prefer primary records (original papers, archives, standards, institutional records, product documentation) for what was done or said; use authoritative reference works and serious peer-reviewed/history scholarship for interpretation and context. For current use, favour current official documentation, professional bodies, method standards, and documented practitioner communities. Treat tertiary explainers as orientation, not the only support for consequential claims.

Every source ID maps to a direct `http(s)` URL, title, publisher/author, and publication date when known. Record a distinct event date when the claim concerns an event; a publication date is not evidence that the event occurred then. Do not invent, repair, or infer a citation URL.

## Claim review

Open and read each cited source. Check what exact claim it supports, whether it is primary or interpretive, and whether its scope matches the report. A syntactically valid citation is not evidence. Triangulate consequential origin, priority, or "first" claims with independent primary/serious scholarly sources when feasible. Cite disagreements rather than choosing a winner by tone.

Label `scholarly dispute`, `uncertain`, `inference`, or `provisional` plainly. Current-use statements must state an evidence cutoff date and avoid projecting current documentation backward. Do not fabricate sources, quotations, dates, consensus, or access claims.
